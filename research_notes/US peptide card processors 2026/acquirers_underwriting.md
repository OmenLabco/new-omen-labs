# US Acquiring Banks, Direct Processors & High-Risk Payfacs Underwriting Nutra / Supplement / Research-Chemical (Peptide) Card Merchant Accounts — 2026

> Scope: US merchant + US cardholders, CARD merchant accounts only (Visa/Mastercard), RUO peptides + lab supplies, ~$10k/mo, ~$60 AOV. Research date: 2026-10-20.
> Labels used: **VERIFIED CURRENT** = corroborated by primary/independent source dated within scope; **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED** = provider claim or single/secondary source, not independently confirmed; **WEAK-EXCLUDE** = evidence suggests this vertical is outside appetite or the source is unreliable. "requires direct underwriting confirmation" noted where the only way to settle is an application.
>
> Overarching caveat: Almost none of the acquirers/banks publish a per-vertical risk appetite. Underwriting appetite for "peptides" / "research chemicals" is set per-program and per-sponsor-bank, is not public, and changes frequently. Most public text on this vertical is marketing from ISOs/resellers with an incentive to describe the market as hard and themselves as the fix. Treat all provider claims as sales framing until confirmed in a written underwriting approval.

## Q1 — Per acquirer/platform: documented nutra/supplement/peptide/research-chemical appetite (wording + URL + date)

### Takeaway
No US acquirer's public documentation affirmatively names "peptides" or "research chemicals" as *within* appetite; the only explicit card-network/acquirer mentions of "research chemicals" are **restrictions** (Adyen, Stripe, Moneris). Nutraceutical/supplement acceptance is real but sits with specialist ISO-fronted programs (Corepay, PaymentCloud, Instabill, Durango, Easy Pay Direct, Paysafe) whose backing acquirer is almost never disclosed. The single largest independent nutra acquirer, **Humboldt Merchant Services, was permanently banned by the FTC in Sept 2026 from high-risk merchant processing** — a material structural change to this market.

### Cited Findings

**Humboldt Merchant Services (HMS) / 5967 Ventures, LLC — acquirer-adjacent ISO/processor, the long-time nutra specialist**
- HMS "specialized in 'high-risk' merchants, including online sellers of nutraceutical supplements and other products that used free trials to enroll consumers in recurring subscriptions." — [ABA Banking Journal, 2026-09-30](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/09/humboldt-merchant-services-agrees-to-pay-12-million-to-resolve-ftc-act-allegations/)
- FTC case *FTC v. 5967 Ventures LLC* (d/b/a Humboldt Merchant Services), filed ~2026-09-08/10; HMS agreed to pay **$12 million** consumer relief and accept a **permanent bar on processing for four categories of risky merchants** (straw/shell companies; merchants on Mastercard's terminated-merchant blacklist [MATCH/MTF]; merchants already defendants in a public consumer-protection case; newly-formed online sellers with mailbox-only addresses / no processing history / negative-option billing). — [American Banker, 2026-09-10](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bmo-payments-partner-to-pay-12m-for-serving-shell-companies); [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/humboldt-merchant-services)
- Label: **VERIFIED CURRENT** (two independent trade/press sources + FTC). HMS "Performance Marketing" = its internal term for supplements/gadgets sold online via negative-option/free-trial billing; chargebacks exceeded 7% of sales by 2021. — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bmo-payments-partner-to-pay-12m-for-serving-shell-companies)

**Paysafe (direct processor/acquirer, high-risk program)**
- Paysafe's own resource center: "Vendors of nutraceutical products have historically been classed as high-risk businesses by issuers and banks" (chargeback-driven). — [paysafe.com](https://www.paysafe.com/en/resource-center/nutraceuticals-compliance-is-key-for-long-term-growth/)

**Elavon, Fiserv/Fidelity high-risk, Maverick Payments, SignaPay, NMI**

- Label: **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED / unknown**; requires direct underwriting confirmation.

### Inferences
- The practical US path for compliant supplements is a specialist ISO (Corepay / PaymentCloud / Durango / Easy Pay Direct) fronting an undisclosed sponsor bank, gated by LegitScript. For *peptides/RUO research chemicals specifically*, the honest read is that no US card acquirer publicly welcomes them; placements tend to go offshore (Instabill) or depend on LegitScript Healthcare certification + a pharmacy/telehealth-style model.
- Humboldt's permanent FTC ban removes the market's most prominent nutra-free-trial acquirer and will make every remaining sponsor bank more conservative on negative-option/free-trial nutra and anything coded like pharma — directly adverse to a ~$60 AOV RUO-peptide reseller.

### Gaps
- No named current sponsor/acquiring bank for Corepay, PaymentCloud, Durango, Easy Pay Direct, or Paysafe's high-risk program.
- No primary underwriting document obtained for Maverick, SignaPay, Elavon, Fiserv/Fidelity high-risk — their files are behind application/login.

## Q2 — Role classification: direct vs ISO/reseller-only (acquirer/sponsor bank vs processor vs payfac)

### Takeaway
The resellers the user named are mostly ISOs/processors; the actual sponsor banks behind them are rarely disclosed. Of the named entities, true acquirers/sponsor banks are BMO (via Humboldt), NorthAB; true direct acquirers/payfacs are Adyen, Stripe, PayPal, Shopify, Paysafe, Elavon, Fiserv; NMI is a gateway only.

### Cited Findings
- **Humboldt / 5967 Ventures = ISO / processor, NOT the bank.** Its **sponsor bank was BMO** "under an agreement dating to December 2009," with Humboldt responsible for "risk/fraud monitoring"; a Humboldt spokesperson says it is "no longer a registered BMO ISO." **NorthAB (formerly North American Bancard, Troy, MI)** is a "corporate affiliate" and also held a BMO sponsorship; in Oct 2020 Humboldt moved its nutra ("Performance Marketing") BIN to NorthAB. — [American Banker, 2026-09-10](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bmo-payments-partner-to-pay-12m-for-serving-shell-companies)
- **BMO** = the sponsor/acquiring bank (not a defendant; FTC alleged no wrongdoing by BMO). **VERIFIED CURRENT.**
- **Corepay = ISO/processor** with its own gateway (Netvalve), reaching merchants through undisclosed acquirers. — [corepay.net](https://corepay.net/articles/legitscript-payment-processing-company/). **VERIFIED** (role) / sponsor undisclosed.
- **Instabill, Durango, PaymentCloud, Easy Pay Direct, Inovio = ISOs/resellers** (reachable only through their program, backing acquirer undisclosed; Instabill peptides = offshore bank). — provider sites above.
- **NMI = payment gateway**, not an acquirer or underwriter (no merchant underwriting of its own). — inferred from role; no primary NMI underwriting doc found.
- **Adyen, Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments = payfacs/direct** (aggregated acceptance under their own master agreements). **Paysafe, Elavon, Fiserv = direct processors/acquirers.**

### Inferences
- "Who sits behind the reseller" for the compliant-supplement path is almost always a mid-tier US sponsor bank the ISO will not name until underwriting; historically BMO (via Humboldt/NorthAB) was a dominant one for nutra and has now stepped back from that high-risk book.

### Gaps
- Current sponsor banks for the surviving nutra ISOs are not publicly documented. T1 Payments' historical US sponsor path ran through Payvision (now wound down) — see Q3.

## Q4 — Card-network context: Visa VIRP, Mastercard rules, MATCH/TMF risk

### Takeaway
Visa moved high-brand-risk from a binary flag (GBPP) to a tiered **Visa Integrity Risk Program (VIRP, effective 2023-05-01)**; drug/pharmacy MCCs (5122/5912) sit in the **highest/Tier-1** bucket. Neither network names "research chemicals" as a product ban; both rely on a **legality + brand-damage** standard (Mastercard BRAM). MATCH/TMF listing is the acute risk for this vertical — Humboldt's own ban explicitly bars serving MATCH-listed merchants.

### Cited Findings
- **Visa:** GBPP ran through 2023-04-30; **VIRP replaced it 2023-05-01**, introducing three risk tiers. MCC 5122 (drugs/proprietaries) and 5912 (pharmacies) are placed in the top tier "alongside gambling and adult content"; VIRP registration fee cited at **USD $950**; drug/pharmacy codes "must also be separated for card-absent sales." — [g2risksolutions.com](https://g2risksolutions.com/resources/blog/visa-replaces-global-brand-protection-program/); [legitscript.com MCC guide](https://www.legitscript.com/regulatory-and-card-brand-compliance/merchant-category-codes); [binderr.com](https://binderr.com/marketplace/high-risk-payment-processors-for-peptides). Mix of **VERIFIED CURRENT** (program dates/tiers) and **PLAUSIBLE** (fee/tier placement, secondary sources).
- Visa Merchant Data Standards Manual: MCC 5122 description/included-services were revised "to align with the updated Visa High Integrity Risk MCCs" (effective date not in snippet). — [visa.com MDS manual](https://www.visa.com.au/dam/VCOM/download/merchants/visa-merchant-data-standards-manual.pdf). **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED.**
- **Mastercard (BRAM / Business Risk Assessment and Mitigation):** prohibits any transaction that is "illegal, or … deemed by Mastercard in its sole discretion, to damage the goodwill of Mastercard," and any sale "other than in full compliance with all applicable laws." No named "research chemicals" ban — the test is legality + brand risk. Current Mastercard Rules manual dated 2025-06-03. — [mastercard.com Rules PDF](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/shared/business/support/rules-pdfs/mastercard-rules.pdf). **VERIFIED CURRENT** (standard), exact merchant-prohibition wording not fully retrieved.
- **MATCH/TMF:** Humboldt's FTC ban explicitly forbids processing for "merchants on Mastercard's shared blacklist of terminated businesses." A MATCH listing (typically 5 years) following a termination for excessive chargebacks or fraud is the dominant structural risk and effectively locks a merchant out across acquirers. — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/bmo-payments-partner-to-pay-12m-for-serving-shell-companies). **VERIFIED CURRENT** (that MATCH is the gating blacklist).
- **LegitScript & networks:** Visa/Mastercard recognize LegitScript certification in the high-risk registration process for MCCs 5122 and 5912 (pharmacy/telemedicine) — **not** a documented supplement requirement; a separate supplement certification track exists; processors (not networks directly) typically impose it for supplements. — [legitscript.com healthcare guide](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare-101-guide); [convesio.com](https://convesio.com/knowledgebase/article/legitscript-certification-guide/). **VERIFIED CURRENT** (pharmacy/telehealth recognition).
- LegitScript: peptide ads flagged as problematic rose **308% in 2024 vs 2023**. — [binderr.com](https://binderr.com/marketplace/high-risk-payment-processors-for-peptides). **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED.**

### Inferences
- A US RUO-peptide seller will likely be coded into 5122-adjacent high-integrity-risk territory, triggering VIRP registration, LegitScript scrutiny, and separation of card-absent volume — i.e., treated like a drug seller, the networks' most restricted tier. Absence of a named "research chemicals" ban is cold comfort: BRAM's "sole discretion / brand goodwill" clause lets any acquirer or the networks deregister the vertical at will.

### Gaps
- Exact current VIRP tier table and whether $950 fee / MCC 5122 "highest tier" placement is current 2026 text — relies on secondary sources; confirm in Visa's VIRP Guide.

## Q5 — Published fees / reserves / settlement terms

### Takeaway
No in-scope US acquirer publishes firm fees/reserves for this vertical; everything available is vendor-estimate range or "not publicly disclosed."

### Cited Findings
- Vendor-cited (not acquirer-published) ranges for peptide/high-risk: card discount ~3.5%–8% per transaction; **rolling reserves 5–10% (some say 10%) held 120–180 days (6–12 months)**; setup $0–$500; monthly $25–$50; chargeback fee $20–$35; PCI $99–$200/yr. — [binderr.com](https://binderr.com/marketplace/high-risk-payment-processors-for-peptides); peptide vendor summaries via [search](https://techbullion.com/the-best-peptide-payment-gateway-in-2026-how-companies-accept-visa-and-mc-with-crypto-settlement/). **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED** (vendor figures, wide variance).
- Paysafe high-risk (CBD example, third-party): quote-based interchange-plus, rolling reserve 5–10% held 6 months, ~2-year contracts, "opaque" pricing. — [sonary.com](https://sonary.com/reviews/paysafe/). **PLAUSIBLE — CONFIRMATION NEEDED.**
- Visa VIRP registration **$950** (card-network fee, not acquirer margin). — [binderr.com](https://binderr.com/marketplace/high-risk-payment-processors-for-peptides). **PLAUSIBLE.**

### Inferences
- For a ~$10k/mo / ~$60 AOV book, expect a reserve hold (5–10% ≈ $500–$1,000/mo escrowed 6 months), discount rates roughly 2–3x standard ecommerce, plus per-chargeback fees that bite hard at this low volume — and realistic placement only through an ISO, not a direct name-brand acquirer.

### Gaps
- No acquirer-published (first-party) fee schedule for peptides/nutra exists in public; all true terms "not publicly disclosed — requires direct underwriting confirmation."
